概览 (Overview)

Rows of terracotta soldiers in an archaeological pit / 考古坑中成排的兵马俑
Emperor Qin Shi Huang and the Great Wall / 秦始皇与长城
Left: Original painted warrior / 彩绘兵俑; Right: Faded terracotta warrior / 褪色兵俑
The mythical interior of the Emperor's tomb / 传说中的皇帝陵墓内部

In 1974, farmers in China accidentally discovered a huge underground army of more than 8,000 life-size clay soldiers. This army was created for Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of China, who came to power in 246 BCE. He was a powerful and ambitious ruler who united China's seven kingdoms. During his reign, he standardized weights, measures, and the writing system, and also started building what would later become the Great Wall. Despite these great achievements, the emperor was obsessed with his own mortality and constantly searched for a way to live forever. He began constructing his massive tomb complex in the first year of his reign, wanting an army to protect and serve him in the afterlife.

The Terracotta Army stands in battle formation in several large pits, complete with soldiers, war chariots, and horses. Other nearby chambers contain figures of musicians, officials, and animals, showing the emperor's detailed plans for the next world. Each statue was individually made from terracotta clay by thousands of artisans, and they were originally painted with bright colors. The actual tomb of Qin Shi Huang remains unopened because archaeologists are concerned that exposure to air would damage the treasures inside. It is said to contain palaces, precious stones, and even rivers of mercury. While many ancient cultures buried items or people with their dead, Emperor Qin's decision to use clay figures instead of human sacrifices marked a significant change from earlier practices. Today, these silent soldiers are a world-famous attraction.
