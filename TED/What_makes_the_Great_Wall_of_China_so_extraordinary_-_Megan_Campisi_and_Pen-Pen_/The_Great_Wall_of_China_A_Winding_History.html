The Great Wall as a modern tourist site / 作为现代旅游景点的长城

The Great Wall of China wasn't built all at once. It began over two thousand years ago as many separate walls made of rammed earth. Different feudal states built them for protection. In 221 BCE, Emperor Qin Shi Huang unified China and started connecting these walls to create a single, massive defense system against northern invaders. This enormous project required hundreds of thousands of workers, including soldiers, commoners, and criminals, many of whom were forced into labor. Under later dynasties like the Han, the wall was extended even further, but its construction became known for the immense suffering and death of its builders, leading to legends of workers being buried within the structure itself.

The most famous sections of the Great Wall we see today were mostly built during the Ming Dynasty (1368-1644). They reinforced the wall using brick and stone, making it taller and stronger, and added watchtowers for signaling with fire and smoke. However, after the Qing Dynasty came to power, the empire's borders expanded beyond the wall, and it lost its military purpose, falling into disrepair. Today, the Great Wall is a UNESCO World Heritage site and a symbol of China, attracting millions of visitors. Although it's a myth that you can see it from the moon, it remains one of history's most incredible engineering achievements—a monument that was built to keep people out but now welcomes the world.
